Bulk uses in practice

Unprinted golf balls in large quantities have far more uses than simply "stock". On a driving range everything revolves around volume and repetition: range balls wear out, go missing and need topping up periodically. Genuine branded balls as a base stock give a better striking feel than the cheapest range balls and generally last longer, which can ultimately lower the cost per round played. For the sales counter of that same range, blank top brands are ideal to resell by the sleeve to players who run short of a ball mid-round.

Golf schools, clinics and corporate sports days also get through balls quickly. At a clinic where dozens of participants practise at once, you simply want enough stock without worrying about printing costs or minimum designs — it is purely about playing. And for clubs stocking their shop without a logo, bulk without printing is the fastest and most economical route to filled shelves. Tell Mark what use you have in mind via contact, and he will help with the right model and quantity for your situation.

Resale: why genuine brands still matter

For anyone reselling golf balls, the brand name on the box is half the story. Golfers know Titleist and Pinnacle and know what those balls give them; that makes selling easier than with an unknown brand, even when the ball is blank. A genuine ball without a logo keeps its branded packaging and recognisability, which builds confidence with the end customer and supports your margin. You do not have to explain the brand — its reputation does that for you.

In bulk, quality matters even more than on a single purchase, because a disappointing batch multiplies the problem by hundreds of balls at once. By working exclusively with genuine Titleist and Pinnacle balls, you avoid complaints about playing characteristics or durability and keep your customers happy. Want to move to a logo later, for instance for your own shop line? Then the switch to printed golf balls is easily made, with the same brand quality as a base. Do compare the models via the ball selector before committing to a large batch.

Choosing the right quantity

With bulk it comes down to the balance between buying advantage and storage. The larger your order, the better the price per ball — but a huge stock sitting idle for months takes up space and ties up money you could use elsewhere. So start with a realistic estimate of your usage per month or per season, and add a safety margin for busy periods or unexpected demand, so you never have to turn a sale away.

For a driving range you work from the turnover rate of your balls; for a shop or resale, from your expected sales per quarter. If you are torn between a medium and a large order, put both scenarios forward: Mark is happy to work through the volume savings so you can see where the tipping point lies and which choice pays off best. We always confirm the available models and quantities in the tailored quotation to confirm. Send your requirements via contact for a concrete proposal that fits your purchasing pattern. It is often wise to start with a quantity you are certain to use within a single season, and only scale up once your usage has proven itself in practice.

Logistics and regular resupply

Anyone who regularly needs large quantities benefits from predictability. Rather than placing one-off rush orders each time, it pays to agree a recurring rhythm — for example a fixed delivery each quarter, or a seasonal order just before the peak. That way you avoid running out of balls on your busiest days and keep the buying price low by ordering in larger batches rather than repeatedly topping up small amounts.

Allow a delivery time of 5 to 15 working days, and for unprinted bulk bear in mind the availability of the chosen model. By sharing your annual planning in advance, Mark can help think through timing and stock build-up, so deliveries dovetail neatly with your season. For larger, structural arrangements a tailored quotation is the handiest route — request one via our quotation form and we will set out the framework clearly in one go.

When do you switch to printed?

Unprinted is ideal as long as it is purely about volume and playing enjoyment. But the moment visibility, recognition or brand experience comes into play, printing is the logical next step. A club logo on the shop ball, a sponsor logo at a tournament or a company logo as a corporate gift: at moments like these the print adds value that blank balls cannot offer, because the ball then also becomes a means of communication.

The neat part is that the base stays the same — you print exactly the same genuine Titleist and Pinnacle balls you also buy blank. Many customers combine both: a large blank stock for everyday use and a printed series for special occasions. Want to explore the possibilities of a logo? Take a look at popular models such as the printed Pinnacle Rush or the Titleist TruFeel, and you will see at once which feel goes with which model.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum order?
The minimum order is 144 golf balls, for example 6 boxes of 24 or 12 dozen. Larger quantities earn tiered discounts.
Do I get a digital proof first?
Yes, always. You receive a digital proof of your logo on the ball and production only starts after your approval.
How long does delivery take?
Allow 5 to 15 working days after you approve the digital proof.
How many colours can I print?
Golf balls are printed in 1 to 5 spot colours. Provide your brand colours as PMS/Pantone values for the best match.
How do I supply my logo?
Preferably as a vector file (EPS, AI or PDF) so the logo comes out perfectly crisp. A high-resolution PNG can work for simple logos.
Can I play with printed golf balls?
Yes. They are original branded balls with a professional print, so they play exactly like unprinted balls.
