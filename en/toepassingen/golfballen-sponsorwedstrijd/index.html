Application · Sponsorship

Printed golf balls for a sponsored competition

As a sponsor you want to be seen where it counts. A logo on the golf ball puts your brand on the course with every player, every hole — visible, measurable and at a low cost per contact.

Start printing → Ask Mark for advice

Sponsoring is investing in visibility, and at a golf competition the ball is the most-used object of the day. Put your sponsor logo on the golf balls and your brand literally plays along in the hands of every participant — a sponsor message that does not stand on the sidelines but travels the whole round. This page explains how to use golf balls to get the most return from your contribution as a sponsor.

MrGolfbal.nl prints exclusively on original Titleist and Pinnacle golf balls with your logo, text or design. You always receive a digital proof first, so you see exactly how your sponsor logo looks on the ball before production starts. Founder Mark Reynolds is a PGA professional and helps you with the ball choice and the layout of your sponsor message.

Maximum visibility for your sponsor logo

A sponsor board along the fairway is seen once; a golf ball is in view the whole round. That is the difference that makes golf balls such a powerful sponsorship tool. The participant looks at the ball on every tee, every green and at every putt — hour after hour. Your sponsor logo travels across the course instead of staying in one spot, and returns again and again into the player's field of view.

For a sponsor standing out among other partners, that is a distinctive advantage. Where many sponsors opt for a board or flag in a fixed position, you choose the tool the player literally holds in hand. That way your brand sticks, even after the competition ends and the ball disappears into the bag.

Low cost per contact

Sponsorship is judged on reach, and golf balls score strongly there. A participant holds their ball for a whole round — add up the holes, the strokes and the glances at the ball, and you reach hundreds of contact moments per player. Spread the cost of the printed balls across all those moments and the cost per contact comes out low compared with many other sponsor messages.

That effect improves the more balls you order: for larger runs the price per ball falls thanks to tiered pricing. Calculate your scenario in the configurator to see how your contribution translates into visibility per euro. The minimum order for printing is 144 golf balls.

Combining sponsor logo and tournament logo

At a sponsored competition the ball need not carry only your logo. You can combine your sponsor logo with that of the tournament or the organising club, so the ball becomes a genuine competition keepsake in which your brand runs firmly alongside. Participants experience that as a polished whole rather than loose advertising — and your name sits on a par with the event.

Printing is done in spot colours, from 1 to 5 colours, so several logos side by side in different colours are readily achievable. Supply your logos as a vector file (EPS, AI or PDF) for the sharpest result. Read how many colours you can print on a golf ball and how best to supply your logo.

Which ball does a sponsor choose?

As a sponsor you want visibility at a sensible price, so a ball with a favourable balance between quality and cost is the logical choice. The Pinnacle Rush is a sporty, lively ball that goes down well with participants and is attractively priced in larger quantities. The Pinnacle Soft is popular too: soft feel, large printing surface and a keen price — ideal for showing off your sponsor logo generously.

Want to link your brand to the highest level, for instance at a prestigious competition? The Titleist Pro V1 gives your sponsor logo a premium stage: the most-played ball on tour, with the status to match. Not sure which ball suits your sponsorship goal? Take the golf ball finder or talk it through with Mark.

Planning around the competition date

A sponsor message has to be ready on time — delivering late is not an option once the competition is fixed. So bear the lead time in mind. After supplying your logo you first receive a digital proof; only after your go-ahead does production start, followed by a delivery time of 5 to 15 working days. So lock in your quantities and ball choice well before the competition date.

Match the quantity to the field: a sleeve of three per player is common, with a margin for the organisers and any prizes. Unsure about packaging or numbers? Read how many golf balls go in a box or dozen. That way your sponsor message is guaranteed ready on the day itself.

Request your quote via the business quote form or see the options on printing golf balls for businesses. Negotiating the ball into your sponsor package

As a sponsor, you decide how to deploy your contribution, and the golf ball is one of the strongest cards you can put on the table. When assembling your package, explicitly ask the organiser whether your logo can go on the match ball — that is often more valuable than an extra board in a fixed spot. Where a board stays put, the ball travels the whole round in every player's hands.

Set out which logo goes where: your sponsor logo alone, or combined with the tournament logo into a genuine match keepsake. Printing is done in one to five spot colours, so several logos side by side are readily achievable. Model your scenario in the configurator and, for a tidy proposal, request a tailored quote.

Ball sponsorship versus board, flag or hole

Sponsor packages usually offer a range of options, and it helps to know where the ball stands apart.

  • Board along the fairway: visible in one fixed spot, usually passed once per round.
  • Flag or hole board: eye-catching, but tied to that single hole.
  • Printed ball: travels with the player across the whole course and returns to view on every shot, tee and putt — for hours on end.

It is precisely that portable, repeated visibility that sets the ball apart from the usual sponsor items. To link your brand to the highest level, the Titleist Pro V1 gives your logo a premium platform; for broad visibility at a keen price, the Pinnacle Rush works well. Not sure of your choice? Try the golf ball selector.

You need not choose between ball and board, of course. Many sponsors combine the two: the board for name recognition in a fixed spot, the ball for the personal, travelling visibility in the player's hands. That way you cover both the broad overview and the repeated point of contact, and each item reinforces the other.

Activation: getting more from your printed ball

A sponsor logo on the ball is strong, but you get more from it by tying a small moment to it. Think of a nearest-the-pin on a hole where your balls are handed out, with a prize from your brand. Or attach a QR promotion to the accompanying sleeve, so players can redeem or discover something after the round.

That turns the ball from a passive statement into an active point of contact that players remember. To broaden the activation across the whole event, the golf event page offers starting points. If you link your sponsorship to a good cause, charity golf follows on nicely. For the crispest result, supply your logo as a vector file with PMS/Pantone colours.

Measuring return and following up

Sponsorship grows more valuable when you make the effect visible. With a printed ball, that is harder to capture in hard figures than a clickable online item, but not impossible. Work out an indication of your contact moments in advance — a player holds the ball for a whole round, amounting to hundreds of glances per person — and set that against your contribution to get a feel for the cost per contact.

After the match, gauge with the organiser how the balls landed and whether players mentioned your brand. Signals like these help you decide whether to renew or expand next year. Because the price per ball falls with larger volumes, expanding often works out more favourably — a bigger field or a second match lowers your cost per contact further. Note your findings briefly and you go into next season's sponsor talks from a position of strength. Feel free to discuss the options via contact.

Dos and don'ts for sponsors

A few rules of thumb ensure your sponsor statement scores rather than fades.

  • Do: keep your logo recognisable and uncluttered; in the limited space of a ball, simplicity works best.
  • Do: match the quantity to the field, with a margin for the organisers and prizes — usually a sleeve of three per player.
  • Don't: start too late. With a delivery time of 5 to 15 working days after proof approval, you want to lock in well before the match date.
  • Don't: settle for a nameless ball; on a genuine Titleist or Pinnacle, your logo signals quality rather than cheap advertising.
