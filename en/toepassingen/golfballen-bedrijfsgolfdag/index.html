Printed golf balls for your company golf day

A company golf day is about experience and visibility. Golf balls carrying your company logo give the day a polished, professional feel — and guests literally take them home.

Start printing → Ask Mark for advice

A company golf day is one of the strongest moments to put your brand in front of clients, relations and staff. Where a banner ends up in a container afterwards, a printed golf ball stays in the bag — and on the course. This page explains exactly how to use golf balls with your company logo for a successful day: from quantities and brand choice to timing and how to request them.

MrGolfbal.nl prints exclusively on original Titleist and Pinnacle golf balls with your logo, text or design. You always receive a digital proof first, so you know exactly how the result looks before production starts. And because founder Mark Reynolds is a PGA professional, you get advice that goes beyond printing: which ball suits which player, and what makes an impression on your guests.

Why printed golf balls work at a company golf day

A golf day is, by definition, a day when your guests spend hours in contact with your brand. A printed golf ball reinforces that in three ways. First, it is functional: the ball is actually used, hole after hole, so your logo appears again and again. Second, it is lasting: unlike a drink or a giveaway that disappears in a drawer at home, a good golf ball keeps going out on the course — even after your event. And third, it is personal: a ball with a logo feels like a considered gesture, not a throwaway.

For companies inviting relations, that is exactly the tone you want to set: polished, sporty and detail-minded. Want to go a step further? Personalise per flight or per guest with a name — see personalise golf balls.

How many golf balls do you need for a golf day?

First work out how many participants you expect. A common choice is a sleeve of three balls per person; to be more generous, give a dozen (twelve) per guest. The minimum order for printing is 144 golf balls — for example 48 sleeves of three, or twelve dozen. For larger fields the price per ball becomes more favourable thanks to tiered pricing.

A handy rule of thumb: order a little more than the exact number of participants. That gives you margin for last-minute sign-ups and a small stock left over for the winners, the organisers or a future occasion. Unsure about numbers? Calculate your run in the configurator or read about how many golf balls go in a box or dozen.

Which golf ball should you choose for the company golf day?

The right ball depends on your guests and your budget. For a mixed field of varying levels, a keenly priced, pleasant-playing ball like the Pinnacle Soft is ideal: soft feel, large printing surface and an attractive price in larger quantities. To surprise your relations with a premium experience, choose a tour ball like the Titleist Pro V1 — the most-played ball on tour, which carries real status with your logo.

Somewhere in between, the Titleist TruFeel is a fine middle ground: the Titleist brand at an accessible price. Not sure which fits? Take the golf ball finder or put your situation to Mark — he is happy to advise on ball choice per audience.

Timing: plan well ahead of your golf day

Do not underestimate the lead time. After supplying your logo you first receive a digital proof for approval; only after your go-ahead does production start. Then allow a delivery time of 5 to 15 working days. In the busy spring and summer — peak season for company golf days — it is wise to submit your request well in advance, certainly for larger runs or special requirements.

A practical plan: lock in your quantities and brand choice at least three to four weeks before the golf day, so there is comfortable time for the proof, production and delivery. That way everything is ready well before the first ball is struck.

How to supply your logo

For the sharpest result, supply your company logo as a vector file (EPS, AI or PDF). If you only have a JPEG or PNG, we can often still work with it — Mark will honestly tell you whether the quality is sufficient. Golf balls are printed in spot colours (1 to 5 colours); preferably provide your brand colours as PMS/Pantone values. Want to know more? A run-of-show: from first idea to first tee in eight weeks

A successful corporate golf day lives or dies by its planning, and the printed balls are one of the first things you lock in — precisely because there is a lead time attached. A simple countdown keeps you on top of it without the last-minute panic.

  • Week 8–6: estimate your field, set the budget and settle on the ball. Request a tailored quote so you can schedule the order.
  • Week 6–4: supply your logo, review the digital proof and approve it. Production only starts once you sign off.
  • Week 4–2: delivery within 5 to 15 working days, leaving room for late sign-ups.
  • Week 2–0: split the balls across sleeves, flights and the prize table, and double-check everything.

By locking in the printing early, you keep the final weeks free for the things that always overrun: catering, flight pairings and the speakers. Unsure about the numbers? Model your scenario in the configurator.

Budgeting: what do printed balls cost within your day's figures?

A corporate golf day carries many line items — green fees, catering, prizes, perhaps a speaker — and the golf balls are a modest but highly visible part of that. The clever bit is that, unlike most items, the ball keeps paying off after the day: it goes into the bag and your logo is still in play weeks later.

When budgeting, work from a sleeve of three balls per player as your baseline and a dozen for special guests or winners. Because the price per ball falls as you order more, it pays to order slightly above your exact field. Precise rates depend on model and volume to confirm — so always request a concrete quote. For affordable playability, look to the Pinnacle Soft; to impress a select group, the Titleist Pro V1 lifts the experience to tour level.

Presentation on the day: where do you place the balls?

Printed balls only land when they appear at the right moment. Three spots work every time. At check-in: lay out a sleeve per player on the registration desk, so guests immediately sense the day has been thought through. On the first tee: a basket of printed balls as a starting touch sets the tone for the round. At the prize table: a full dozen as the prize for longest drive or nearest-to-pin looks far more polished than an off-the-shelf gift.

If you want the day to be part of a wider package, a printed ball pairs neatly with other give-aways in a welcome bag — take a look at how a goodie bag is put together. And if you are considering sending balls to clients as a thank-you afterwards, the golf balls as a corporate gift page follows on seamlessly.

Dos and don'ts for a polished golf day

A few practical lessons make the difference between a tidy day and a genuinely professional one.

  • Do: supply your logo as a vector file (EPS, AI or PDF) and give brand colours as PMS/Pantone values. That way the print in one to five spot colours comes out crisp.
  • Do: order a small buffer above your field for no-shows who turn up anyway, reserve flights and the organisers.
  • Don't: wait until the final week. With a delivery time of 5 to 15 working days after proof approval, you want to start in good time.
  • Don't: pick the priciest model for a mixed field when most players will barely notice — match the ball to your guests.

Not sure which ball suits your field? Try the golf ball selector or put your situation to us via contact. As a PGA professional, Mark will help you choose per audience.

Afterwards: measuring impact and preparing the next edition

The golf day is over, but the printed ball only really does its work in the weeks that follow — every time a player tees it up. To make the effect tangible, ask briefly about the experience in your thank-you email, or check with your account managers whether the day comes up in conversation with clients. Signals like these say more about your day's value than a single figure.

If you have a small stock of balls left over, keep them deliberately: they are ideal as a follow-up gift for those who could not attend, or as a warm-up for the next edition.
