A firm ball for competition day

The Rush has a firmer core than the Soft and is aimed at distance. Where the Soft targets feel, the Rush targets speed off the club. For players with a brisk swing that feels better; for those who swing gently, the ball can come across as hard.

On a competition day with a mixed field that difference rarely decides the result, but it does shape how the ball feels off the first tee. That is precisely the moment participants form their opinion of the gift in their goodie bag.

Sponsor logos on the Rush

The white surface holds a crisp print well, including the high-contrast sponsor colours that need to stay legible from a distance. For a sponsor competition that is a real advantage. If you split the run across several sponsors, bear in mind that the minimum of 144 balls applies per print — more on that in printing in small runs.

Who the printed Pinnacle Rush suits best

The Pinnacle Rush is the distance ball within the Pinnacle family: a firm core aimed at extra yards, at a keen price that makes it ideal as a tournament ball. As a printed ball, the Rush suits players looking for length off the tee who appreciate a firm, energetic response at the clubface. For a tournament, a matchplay day or a competitive corporate event, that is exactly the profile that matches the mood.

Where the Pinnacle Soft aims for comfort and a soft feel, the Rush goes for firmness and distance. Both are keenly priced and therefore suited to larger runs; the choice is mainly about the character you want to project. To make that call well, read Pinnacle Soft vs Rush in the knowledge base or use the golf ball selector. An overview of the full range is on printed Pinnacle golf balls.

Playing characteristics explained: what firm and distance mean

The Rush is designed around speed and distance, with a firmer core. What does that mean in practice for the player who receives the ball?

  • Firm feel — at impact the Rush gives a powerful, direct response. Players who like a snappy feel experience that as confirmation they have struck the ball well.
  • Distance off the tee — the speed-built core helps the ball run away on the longer shots, which makes the model popular with those who like to gain yards.
  • High flight with lower spin — the ball climbs well into the air and, thanks to the lower spin, holds a stable, straight line. Mishits drift less far offline, which brings calm on a tournament day.

The result is a sporty, performance-minded ball that plays pleasantly for a broad audience, from the keen amateur to the occasional player who mainly wants to score on distance. It is precisely that blend of length and a forgiving, straight flight that makes the Rush suited to a competition field of mixed abilities. You will find more model-by-model guidance on the Pinnacle overview page.

Why the Rush excels as a tournament ball

At a tournament you want a ball that looks tidy, plays competitively and still stays affordable across the whole field. The Pinnacle Rush unites all three: its firm, distance-oriented character fits the competitive mood, while the keen price makes it possible to supply every flight or every player with printed balls.

With your logo or the sponsor's on it, the Rush also becomes a tangible memento of the day — a ball players often keep and later play again. That way the visibility of your brand or event keeps running long after the prize-giving. For the organisation of a golf tournament, that is an efficient way to combine sponsorship and experience. The Rush also performs excellently at a competitive corporate golf day. Put your logo on the ball via the configurator.

How your logo looks on the Pinnacle Rush

Like the other Pinnacle balls, the Rush has a smooth, even white surface that holds a crisp print well. Thanks to the model's sporty, competitive character, strong, high-contrast brand colours come across particularly well here — think of a sponsor logo that is instantly legible even at a distance. We print in one to five spot colours; supply your colours as PMS/Pantone values for the closest match.

With a tournament ball it is common to want to combine several logos, for example the event's and a main sponsor's. We then look together at the best layout and scale, so everything stays legible and nothing disappears into the dimples. FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When should I choose the Rush over the Soft?
When your group swings briskly and values length over a soft click. The Rush has a firmer core and targets distance. With a group that swings gently, that same firmness can come across as hard. If you cannot tell beforehand, the Soft is the safer choice.
Can one run be split across several sponsors?
It can, but count on 144 balls per separate print. Four sponsors with their own logo therefore means that minimum four times over. A common alternative is a single design carrying all the sponsor logos together; that rarely sits well on a ball, so discuss the layout before fixing the split.
Does the Rush feel hard to putt with?
Firmer than the Soft, yes. Whether that reads as hard depends on where you are coming from: after a tour ball the difference is clear, after another distance ball barely. On a competition day with a mixed field it seldom stands out, because most participants have no direct comparison to hand.
